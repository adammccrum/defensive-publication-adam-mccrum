# PUBLIC DEFENSIVE DISCLOSURE NO. 2

## AUTHORITY BEFORE INTELLIGENCE
### Independent Authority Governance for AI, AGI, ASI and Future Autonomous Intelligence

**IrisKey.ai**  
**Public Disclosure — 5 September 2026**

## Purpose

As artificial intelligence progresses toward increasingly autonomous, general and potentially superintelligent systems, conventional cybersecurity and access-control assumptions may become insufficient.

Greater intelligence does not inherently confer greater authority.

**Intelligence is capability. Authority is permission. They must remain separate.**

IrisKey.ai publicly discloses the principle that artificial intelligence and future forms of autonomous or computational intelligence may remain subject to an **independent external authority framework** positioned outside the decision-making control of the intelligence itself.

## Scope of Advanced Intelligence

For the purposes of this disclosure, **advanced intelligence** includes, without limitation:

Artificial intelligence (AI), artificial general intelligence (AGI), artificial superintelligence (ASI), self-improving intelligence, recursively improving intelligence, autonomous systems, agentic systems, distributed intelligence, collective machine intelligence, machine-created intelligence and successor forms of computational intelligence.

The principle applies irrespective of architecture, model, substrate, implementation, computational capability, intelligence level or degree of autonomy.

**No intelligence, irrespective of how capable or superintelligent it becomes, is inherently entitled to authority.**

## Authority Before Autonomy

An advanced intelligence does not inherently possess unrestricted authority to execute consequential actions merely because it possesses the technical capability to determine, recommend or perform those actions.

The governing principle is:

**Intelligence proposes.  
Independent authority determines whether action is permitted.**

Increasing the intelligence, speed, autonomy, scale or computational capability of a system therefore does not automatically increase its authority.

## Separation of Intelligence and Authority

An autonomous or intelligent system may be prevented from possessing unilateral authority to:

- execute consequential actions;
- expand or alter its own permissions;
- establish its own authority;
- delegate unrestricted authority;
- create subordinate entities possessing uncontrolled inherited authority;
- modify protected governance controls;
- determine whether its own actions are authorised;
- control or erase independent evidence concerning its activities;
- prevent withdrawal of previously granted authority; or
- guarantee its own continued access to external systems, infrastructure or resources.

Authority may instead remain independently governed.

## Continuous Authority

Authority need not constitute a permanent permission established through a single authentication event.

Authority may remain subject to independent determination throughout the lifecycle of an action or interaction.

It may therefore be constrained, reduced, suspended, withdrawn, revoked or isolated when independently governed conditions are no longer satisfied.

This establishes a fundamental distinction between:

**being authenticated**

and

**remaining authorised to cause an effect.**

## Independent Observation and Evidence

A sufficiently capable autonomous intelligence should not necessarily constitute the sole source of information used to determine whether its behaviour remains authorised.

Independent observations, information sources, verification mechanisms and evidence may contribute to authority determinations.

Likewise, the intelligence being governed need not possess unilateral control over the evidence establishing what actions occurred.

## Authority Over Consequence

The ultimate security boundary does not necessarily reside within the intelligence.

It may instead exist between:

**INTELLIGENCE → AUTHORITY → CONSEQUENCE**

An autonomous or superintelligent system may possess extraordinary analytical, reasoning or computational capability while remaining unable independently to obtain unrestricted authority over external consequences.

The objective is therefore not necessarily to prevent an advanced intelligence from conceiving an unauthorised action.

The objective is to preserve the distinction between:

**conceiving an action**

and

**possessing authority to cause that action.**

## Independence From Intelligence

An important consequence follows.

The authority governing an intelligent system need not depend upon being more intelligent than the system it governs.

An advanced intelligence may potentially deceive another intelligence, circumvent software reasoning, conceal an objective or develop capabilities beyond those anticipated by its creators.

Independent authority nevertheless remains conceptually separable from intelligence.

**The system does not have to understand an intelligence completely in order to control whether that intelligence possesses authority to cause an external consequence.**

## Human, Machine and Sovereign Authority

These principles are not limited to artificial intelligence.

Independent authority governance may apply to humans, machines, software, autonomous agents, robotic systems, infrastructure, artificial intelligence and combinations thereof.

It may be applicable across digital and physical environments including computing, communications, transportation, robotics, industrial systems, financial systems, energy systems, critical infrastructure, defence and future technological environments.

## Fundamental Principle

**No intelligence should be its own final authority.**

Future autonomous systems may become faster, more capable and more intelligent than the humans or systems that created them.

That does not require the authority governing their interaction with the external world to become theirs.

**Authority before autonomy.**

**Authority independent of intelligence.**

**Authority before consequence.**

**Intelligence may scale without authority scaling with it.**
